# RentRecord — brainstorm and value proposition

## 1. Selected idea

RentRecord is a tenant workspace for household bills, apartment records and moving responsibilities. Planned local benefits connect tenants with nearby restaurants, groceries and services. Its central purpose is to make rental information easier to organise, find and act on.

## 2. Four-quadrant brainstorm

| Features | Target audience |
| --- | --- |
| Structured household bills and monthly overview | Students and young adult tenants |
| Move-in/out photos, condition notes and comparison | International tenants in Finland |
| Move-out checklist and deposit timeline | Tenants managing several household bills |
| Planned local restaurant, grocery and service offers | Local businesses as prospective partners |

| Problem hypotheses | Intended value |
| --- | --- |
| Bills and dates require checking several places | One organised view of household obligations |
| Apartment photos are difficult to retrieve | Room-based records and comparison |
| Moving tasks are easy to overlook | A visible checklist and next actions |
| Relevant local offers are scattered | Offers with clear terms and validity |

## 3. Why the parts belong together

The home is the organising unit. Bills support everyday use; condition records support moving; local benefits offer potential additional savings. The underlying claim is convenience and clarity, not that combining features automatically creates demand.

## 4. Priorities

1. Demonstrate the apartment-record and deposit journey with the submitted code.
2. Validate manual household bills and implement a compact bills overview.
3. Test local offer interest with tenants and prospective businesses before promising rewards.

The initial bills scope is organising and tracking, with payments handled externally. Local benefits are discounts or offers, without a points currency.

## 5. Assumptions and alternatives

Users may already manage these tasks adequately with banking apps, calendars, photos and landlord portals. No claim of market uniqueness or competitor superiority is made. Compare how long participants need to find a bill or an apartment photo using their current method and the concept.

Important assumptions: users accept manual entry; one workspace is more convenient; relevant businesses will participate; benefits are attractive enough to use. No interviews or partner agreements are documented.

## 6. Small validation exercise

Ask five target tenants about their last actual bill-management or moving experience. Observe their current methods before showing the concept. Test whether they can identify unpaid bills and retrieve a room record. Ask prospective businesses what customer reach, redemption controls and reporting they would need. Record disagreements and lack of interest as well as positive feedback.

## 7. Risks and boundaries

Scope must remain manageable. The current prototype covers records and deposit tracking; bills and offers remain planned. No automatic bill imports, payments, verified on-time-payment rewards or guaranteed dispute prevention are claimed. Success for the assignment is a clear problem hypothesis, coherent design, working demonstration and honest evaluation.
